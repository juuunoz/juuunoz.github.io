Okay, so, 5 years of a computer science degree later, I've developed a set of skills congruent with being able to see a portfolio website through development. Hurray! Obviously, there were some technical aspects involved, but also some real changes in mindset that helped me see this project to completion this time around. 

Designing and developing personal projects feels ever so different from building something at a company, and the difference felt palpible for me to write about it here. 

#### TECHNOLOGY
This website is a React-based web app with Typescript, Express middleware, and PostgreSQL database. Stored in the database are notes sent from a Discord bot and then displayed on my website. The Discord bot is written in Python. Everything is hosted on a Digitalocean Droplet, including the bot, and the database, which is containerized using Docker for organizational reasons. The reason I chose the non-traditional path of a Discord bot as my CMS, was because I wanted a casual, low-friction and maybe more intimate way of communicating what i was up to, in a micro-journalling sort of flavour. I also just thought, why not?\
\
Truthfully this project is not exactly the most complicated thing I've ever built, but nonetheless it's a project that I feel encapsulates who I am as a person quite well. Genuine, thoughtful, multi-faceted, keen on learning and keen on sharing what i've learned (not to toot my own horn).

#### ON PLAY 
As has been frequently mentioned by me, this project was largely inspired by the playful and creative website of [computer angel](https://www.computerangel.blog/). I've been very fixated on this idea of incorporated play lately. Play is an action, but it can also be a state of mind. It's something that should be invoked while relaxing and hobbying, and also incorporated into serious work, like studying or professional ventures. While a pretty well-understood concept in the contemporary art world by this point, I find it sorely missed in STEMmier environments related to software development. The main idea is to approach all sorts of work in a relaxed, curious but focused manner. There is open ended, fast and interest-based experimentation, and it's great for both ideation and taking some of the stress out of development work. Integrating play means integrating joy and learning into the process, which generally tends to make said process a lot easier to work on everyday.

But, with play, also should be paired a healthy dose of discipline. It's a common misconception that routines and discipline kill creativity. But I actually think that well-designed boundaries around when to create and when to toil help the creative process by taking away that nagging feeling of when will this end? And, god, I really don't want to do what. 

Play shouldn't be boundless. Usually, things tapered off naturally with my own waning interest, but I've also learned when to put a cork in ideation, having already arrived at a good enough solution and with deadlines fast approaching. Projects with deadlines should be comfortable, but strict. Many times in the past I've cut myself off too early by setting overenthusiastic deadlines and killing momentum by forcing myself to ship boring, cookie-cutter feature after feature. So, giving a wide berth and aiming to finish early is a philosophy I follow in regards to setting deadlines now, and it's worked out quite well for this project indeed.

There were also aspects of development that I didn't quite enjoy and therefore struggled to incorporate play in. Things I didn't want to build, but had to get done to ensure smoother development and more fun in the future. For example, a deployment pipeline was one of said things I dreaded, but nonetheless am thankful for building now. I suppose one way to think of automation is simply making more time to do the fun stuff, which it did, so I'm glad I did that, and as has been the case for other aspects of web development, I'm sure I'll come back to it one day interested in what the Git Actions YAML file is really doing under the hood.

#### BUILDING IN PUBLIC
In total, it took me about a month to make this website (and maybe juuust a little longer to get this writeup going). I posted about it frequently in a closed forum in a creative community of mine. Shortly after getting it live, I did a demo of the New Systems Show & Tell night, and it was well receieved! Demo-ing something I made in front of an audience for the first time was, shockingly, very fun. Despite how simple the whole project was, the passion and pride was there, and I got some very good feedback that you can read more about in the #portfolio tag of this website. Sprouting projects can feel fragile and many people may feel protective of their idea for fear of both disparaging comments and/or copycatting. Despite this, I think building publically as early as possible is a very important aspect of creating. I have the good fortune of being surrounded by an extremely bright and hilarious community of tech and creative professionals in my personal circle, and an even larger, very supportive and helpful community of techies in Toronto. There's always someone smarter than you here, which means there's always gonna be someone that can improve your project in ways that you never would've even guessed. 

Even if no one can help you, finding solace in the universal struggle of code that was working yesterday, but suddenly stopped working today can be it's own source of joy and support. Also. Development tunnel vision is real, and I am very thankful to the people that reminded me about what was most important to the project (displaying all the notes in a clean, reliable and accessible manner) and what wasnt (filtering my dms for swear words).

For my upcoming yet-to-be-named project, I'll be pushing to get demos going as soon as is physically possible, and I hope people rip it to shreds, so I can put it back better than ever.
